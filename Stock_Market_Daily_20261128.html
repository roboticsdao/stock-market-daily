deVere Group — January Effect vs Market Timing: Does Seasonality Work?

The January Effect is a calendar anomaly describing observed stock market rises in the first month of the year, first described in 1942 by investment banker Sidney B. Wachtel who noted small stocks consistently outperformed the market. A Morningstar study of 100 years of data showed US stocks historically rose in January 64% of the time, while December saw stocks rise nearly 80% of the time, partly attributed to the Santa Claus Rally. However, recent data shows the effect has weakened substantially. Investopedia data indicates the SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust enjoyed just 18 winning Januaries in the past 31 years. Invesco found that between 2000 and 2023, the market shrunk by an average of 0.3% in January. Bloomberg data shows the Russell 2000 small-cap index averaged a 1.7% rise in January from the mid-1980s to 2014, but just 0.1% from 2014 to 2025. The January Barometer, introduced by Yale Hirsch in 1972, suggests January performance predicts the entire year.

总结：一月效应是一种日历异常现象，指观察到股票市场在一年的第一个月上涨，最初由投资银行家西德尼·B·瓦希特尔于1942年描述，他指出小型股一直表现优于市场。 晨星公司对100年数据的研究显示，美国股市历史上在1月有64%的时间上涨，而12月有近80%的时间上涨，这部分归因于圣诞老人涨势。 然而，近期数据表明该效应已大幅减弱。 Investopedia的数据表明，SPDR标普500 ETF信托在过去31年中仅经历了18次上涨的一月。 景顺发现，在2000年至2023年期间，市场在1月平均缩水0.3%。 彭博社数据显示，罗素2000小盘股指数在20世纪80年中期至2014年期间1月平均上涨1.7%，但从2014年至2025年仅上涨0.1%。 由耶鲁·赫什于1972年引入的一月晴雨表表明，一月的表现可以预测全年。
