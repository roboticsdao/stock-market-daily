Bruegel — Lessons for the renminbi from the yen internationalisation story

The Japanese yen was the first Asian currency to become a major international reserve currency, with its share of global foreign-exchange reserves rising from under one percent in 1976 to 8.7 percent in 1991, before losing momentum. China initiated renminbi internationalization in 2009 by permitting cross-border trade settlement in renminbi, mirroring Japan's large creditor status, current-account surplus, and mature banking industry. However, the two countries differ critically in their relationships with the United States. While Japan maintained a cooperative relationship under US pressure by opening to foreign investment and accepting a sharp yen appreciation after the 1985 Plaza Accord for commercial motives, China remains protective of its domestic economy and wary of dollar dependence. Eichengreen et al noted that China's route does not require full capital-account convertibility, spreading through trade settlement, renminbi lending, swap lines, and offshore centers. The yen's share of world reserves later fell to 3.4 percent in 2009 before recovering to 5.8 percent by the end of 2025, supported by Japan's open capital account and deep, hedgeable markets. Conversely, China has taken a faster, different route to internationalization as its global economic presence grew.

总结：日元是第一个成为主要国际储备货币的亚洲货币，其在全球外汇储备中的份额从1976年的不到1%上升到1991年的8.7%，随后势头减弱。 中国在2009年允许跨境贸易以人民币结算，开启了人民币国际化进程，这类似于日本作为大型债权人、拥有庞大经常账户顺差以及成熟银行业的情况。 然而，两国在与美国的关系上存在关键差异。 日本在美方压力下通过对外国投资开放并出于商业动机接受1985年《广场协议》后日元的大幅升值来维持合作关系，而中国则对国内经济保持保护态度并对美元依赖保持警惕。 埃肯格林等学者指出，中国的路径不需要完全的资本账户可兑换，而是通过贸易结算、人民币贷款、央行互换协议和离岸清算中心来传播。 日元在全球储备中的份额后来在2009年跌至3.4%，到2025年底回升至5.8%，这得益于日本开放的资本账户以及深厚且可进行对冲的市场。 相反，随着中国全球经济影响力的增长，中国采取了一条更快且不同的国际化路径。
